# Short-Form Video Script (45–60 seconds)

## Hook — 0:00–0:06

**On screen:** “Does high AI exposure mean a job will disappear?”

**Voiceover:** “The BLS data says: not necessarily.”

## Evidence — 0:06–0:20

**On screen:** `831 detailed U.S. occupations` then `412 High/Very high relative AI exposure`

**Voiceover:** “I analyzed 831 detailed U.S. occupations. Four hundred twelve were classified as having High or Very high relative AI exposure.”

**On screen:** `306 projected to grow` / `100 projected to decline` / `6 at 0% projected change`

**Voiceover:** “But among those 412 occupations, 306 are projected to grow from 2025 to 2035. One hundred are projected to decline.”

## Contrast — 0:20–0:32

**On screen:** `Data scientists: Very high exposure, +34.6% projected growth` then `Data entry keyers: Very high exposure, −25.5% projected growth`

**Voiceover:** “Two occupations can both have very high relative AI exposure and still have very different projected employment paths.”

## Skill result — 0:32–0:45

**On screen:** `Strongest descriptive correlation: Critical & analytical thinking, 0.508`

**Voiceover:** “In this high-exposure group, critical and analytical thinking had the strongest descriptive correlation with projected employment growth. That is an association, not a guarantee or a causal effect.”

## Close — 0:45–0:58

**On screen:** “Ask better questions about AI and work.”

**Voiceover:** “So the useful question is not ‘Which jobs will AI replace?’ It is ‘Why do some highly exposed occupations grow while others decline?’”

**Footer on every frame:** `BLS relative AI exposure is not a measure of job loss or worker replacement. Projections: 2025–2035.`
