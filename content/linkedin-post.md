# LinkedIn Draft

I started with a simple question: “Which jobs will AI replace?”

The BLS data pushed me toward a better question.

Across 831 detailed U.S. occupations, 412 are classified as having High or Very high **relative AI exposure**. Yet 306 of those 412 occupations are projected to grow from 2025 to 2035, while 100 are projected to decline.

That does not mean AI has no effect on employment. It means AI exposure and projected employment decline are not the same measure.

Within the High/Very high exposure group, projected-growth occupations scored higher on average in Science, Critical & analytical thinking, Leadership, Writing & reading, and Problem solving & decision making. Critical & analytical thinking had the strongest descriptive correlation with projected employment growth (0.508).

The important caution: these are occupation-level associations, not proof that a skill causes growth or protects a job.

My takeaway is less about labeling careers and more about asking better questions: How do technology, task mix, skills, industry demand, and regulation interact in an occupation?

Data: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics AI exposure categories, Employment Projections, and Skills Data, 2025–2035.
